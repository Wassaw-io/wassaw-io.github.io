Services

Analysis with a decision at the end

Forecasting

Demand, sales, or staffing forecasts with intervals, backtested against simple baselines so you know the model earns its complexity. Data with gaps, drift, and seasonality included.

Predictive models

Models built, evaluated on data they have not seen, and deployed with input checks and monitoring. Classical methods where they win, which is more often than the current mood suggests.

Model and pipeline audits

Why a model looks good in testing and fails in use: leakage, drift, the wrong metric, or a split that puts the same customer on both sides. You get the cause, the fix, and a test that catches it next time.

LLM features and agents

Retrieval over your own documents, agents and automation (including n8n), and prompt testing, each with an evaluation set so you know whether it works before your customers find out.

Handover

Case studies you can poke at

The churn rate is the wrong number

Survival analysis on student retention, a frailty model for why the churn rate falls when nothing about any student changes, and lifetime value recomputed off the survival curve. Sliders move the assumptions and the decision flips.

Who to call is not who will leave

Targeting retention outreach by churn probability performs worse than random. Uplift modelling on a randomised trial, a Qini curve you can read at any depth, and the intervention cost where targeting starts to pay.

Staff to the top of the forecast, not the middle

A backtested forecast turned into a staffing schedule, with a calculator for your own costs. Scheduling at the right point in the range cut the expected cost of mistakes by 25%.

When to stop tuning, and when to ignore the drift alarm

Model choice with a computed ceiling, a scoring tool that checks its input, and drift monitoring that avoided a retrain the model did not need.

Ranking locations by churn rate ranks the noise

Hierarchical Bayes across fourteen centers, with a toggle between raw and pooled rates. None could be called worse than average.

A 99.97% score in testing, and under 0.1% of new attacks caught

An attack detector tested on attack types it never saw, with every threshold stated as alerts per day, and what 100 labelled examples add.

Customer segments that describe behavior, and pairings that are not just milk

Segmentation and market basket analysis in SQL and Python, with a toggle between raw and scaled segments and a sortable rules table.

Reviews did not predict sales, and they caught a bad batch a month early

Sentiment scoring on 11,700 reviews, a comparison across and within products, and an alarm with a slider for how many false alarms to accept.

One more example

At NASA Wallops Flight Facility I was forecasting local air temperature and modeling the dependence of lightning frequency on local climate change, working in R on data that did not behave the way the textbooks assume. Working through the residuals turned up something that was not a modeling problem at all: a NOAA sensor was malfunctioning. The forecasting work was published with me as first author, and certain results went into a NASA Technical Memorandum.

That is the thing I would want you to know about how I work. The interesting result usually shows up in the part of the analysis that is not behaving, and most of the value is in taking that seriously instead of smoothing it out.
